Technical systems and automation

Final Report · §7.1, Future operational and technical changes

Advanced separation-management tools combine improved trajectory prediction with automated detection of potential conflicts and generation of possible resolutions. SESAR Solution #0468, for example, explores “what-next” resolution proposals covering headings, speeds, levels and direct routings, supported by weather-aware conflict assessment. This can transfer part of the conflict-searching and option-generation workload from the controller to the system. The controller’s work correspondingly shifts towards interpreting predictions, comparing proposals, selecting or modifying

Automatic speech recognition (ASR) converts controller–pilot voice exchanges into structured information. SESAR has explored their use to check issued instructions against system data, maintain command histories, update displays and reduce manual inputs. Complementary attention-guidance tools analyse traffic interactions and visually de-emphasise flights not expected to require immediate spacing action, while highlighting those more likely to need attention. Taken together, these concepts could reduce routine transcription, data entry and undifferentiated display scanning, while increasing the importance of checking machine-generated interpretations and attention cues.

SESAR’s ATC Digital Assistant concept explores the use of AI to support several distinct functions: tactical conflict-resolution advice, identification of flight-efficiency opportunities, short-term traffic forecasting and correction of flight plans. Unlike a tool dedicated to a single task, an assistant could support a wider sequence of analytical and preparatory activities. ATCO work could therefore evolve from performing each analysis separately towards evaluating and supervising a set of machine-generated assessments and proposals. This remains a research-oriented example; its potential effects on operational task allocation should not be presented as established deployment practice.

Nature of the change in ATCO tasks

The concepts can also be read as a progression in the functions assigned to automation, from information handling towards wider operational action. The categories below are analytical rather than formal SESAR capability levels.

Across these examples, we can observe an increased reliance on automation, which progressively supports or performs components of tasks historically carried out by controllers: information acquisition, situation prediction, solution generation, coordination and, at higher levels, action implementation. This suggests a systematic change in the balance between direct task execution and the review, supervision and intervention activities associated with automated systems.

This direction is explicit in the European ATM Master Plan (2025 edition). Its Automation Roadmap envisages ATM at automation level 2 by 2035 and, for certain flight phases and within a predefined operating scope, level 4 by 2045. These are strategic targets, not descriptions of today’s operational baseline. The roadmap’s levels distinguish decision support, resolution support, conditional automation and confined automation for examining task redistribution.

Our key takeaway from this analysis is that this explicit increase in reliance on automation provides an example of a possible transformation of ATCO work. It is also clear even from these very high-level considerations that in order to understand the full scope of the change, it is not enough to look at the work of air traffic controllers in general: the analysis needs to look at individual tasks performed by ATCOs, and how those tasks would change. As a logical next step, we introduce a generic ATCO task taxonomy, which enables a more detailed analysis of the changes induced by increasing automation.

Rather, automation progressively supports or performs components of tasks historically carried out by controllers: information acquisition, situation prediction, solution generation, coordination and, at higher levels, action implementation. The nature and scale of that change remain concept-specific and maturity-dependent.
