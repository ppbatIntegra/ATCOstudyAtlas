Baseline data sources

Final Report · §2.1, Baseline data

Baseline data consist of publicly available European datasets, official publications and other reference sources used to establish a common and comparable evidence base for the study. These sources provide information on traffic demand, operational performance, workforce availability, training activity and workforce planning assumptions across the European ATM network and have been used for modelling purposes.

Table 1: Baseline data sources and information extracted (Source: Consortium assessment)

EUROCONTROL
ACE Benchmarking [1]		Historical workforce and operational data at ANSP and Area Control Centre (ACC) level, including number of ATCOs, flight hours controlled, controller hours on duty, ATCO productivity and trainees in different training phases. Used as the primary source for the historical workforce assessment.

Performance Review [2]		Traffic demand, operational performance and ATFM delay data, including delay attribution. Used to assess the relationship between workforce availability, operational capacity and network performance.

In addition to the European datasets presented above, the study uses a range of national and international reference sources throughout the different analytical activities undertaken. These sources were used to provide regulatory, operational and institutional context, support the interpretation and validation of findings and complement areas where equivalent harmonised European information is not available to support the parameterisation of specific variables.

Workforce and delay data

Intermediate Report · §1.1.1 and §1.1.4

To gain insight into capacity performance issues, planned measures and details on the ATCO situation and ATCO training, the performance plans and annual monitoring reports of the Single European Sky (SES) Member States were analysed from the third and fourth reference periods (RP3 and RP4).

As the practice was introduced in RP3, and data provision was on a voluntary basis (i.e. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/317 does not require Member States to provide this data), some Member States did not provide the complete dataset, limiting their submissions to, for example, the number of ATCOs in OPS at the end of each year. Further to this, data quality was also questionable during the first submissions, as Member States interpreted the calculation of ATCOs in OPS FTEs differently. Both of these limitations improved substantially during RP3, to the extent that the RP4 performance plans provide a reasonably complete dataset with an acceptable data quality.

Given that the performance plans and, especially, the monitoring reports provide information not only on the planned but also on the actual figures, it is possible to compare how successful ANSPs were in realising their plans. Unfortunately, this comparison is currently limited to the RP3 years, as actual figures for 2025 are not available at the time of writing this report. However, the data tables based on this information will be updated after the 1st of June 2026, once Member States submit their 2025 monitoring reports.

Performance plans and annual monitoring reports serve as the basis for collecting information on capacity performance, training practices, ATCO numbers and their evolution (especially comparisons between planned and realised figures) and the number of ATCO students in the training process.

The EUROCONTROL Aviation Intelligence Unit maintains datasets on ATFM delays in the European ATM network, covering both en route and airport arrival delays. These datasets provide daily figures for each entity in the dataset and include the following data:

  • The actual number of IFR movements (or arrivals).
  • The total ATFM delays generated on the day.
  • The breakdown of ATFM delays generated on the day under each delay reason code.
  • The number of IFR flights with delays longer than 15 minutes.

These data are provided either on the ANSP or airport level, depending on the dataset. Datasets are available in two versions: with or without post-operational adjustments. In the study, we use the datasets without the post-operational adjustments as these are better suited for operational analysis. The en route delay dataset covers 2011 to 2025 (including the early months of 2026), while the airport dataset covers only 2019 to 2025 (also including the early months of 2026).

The primary use of these datasets is to identify capacity shortages due to a lack of ATCOs and staffing problems, and to analyse the evolution of these shortfalls.

Model variables

Final Report · Annex F, Model variables definition

The supply-demand model is parameterised using a defined set of variables that determine the evolution of both ATCO demand and ATCO supply over the projection period.

For transparency and traceability, the following summarises the variables incorporated into the model, their definitions, degree of controllability and the principal data sources used for their parameterisation.

Table 40: Variables of the supply-demand model (Source: Consortium assessment)

VariableDefinitionControllabilitySource
DEMAND
Flight controlled hoursThe volume of Instrumental Flight Rules (IFR) traffic handled by an ACC, expressed as flight hours controlled. This variable constitutes the primary driver of demand within the model.External or partially controllableHistorical traffic is derived from the EUROCONTROL ACE benchmarking dataset [1] at ACC level. Future traffic is projected using the EUROCONTROL STATFOR forecast [6] up to 2032 and the EUROCONTROL Aviation Outlook 2050 [7] thereafter (2033-2036), applied as growth rates to the latest observed ACC traffic levels.
ATCO productivityThe amount of traffic that can be managed per hour of controller operational duty, expressed as flight hours controlled per ATCO hours on duty.ControllableBaseline productivity is derived from the EUROCONTROL ACE benchmarking [1] dataset. Future productivity is projected using the annual growth observed over the 2012-2019 period, which is considered representative of pre-pandemic structural productivity improvements. Four productivity scenarios are modelled: baseline, low (50% of baseline growth), SESAR deployment [9] (143% of baseline growth) and Master Plan [2] (200% of baseline growth).
UtilisationThe proportion of available controller duty hours that is effectively dedicated to operational activities. It determines how many of the hours a controller can supply are available for operations, and therefore how many controllers a given number of controller hours requires.ControllableDerived from EUROCONTROL ACE [1] utilisation indicators and cross-referenced with EUROCONTROL Performance Review data [8] on ATFM delay attributed to ATC staffing and capacity constraints. The selected utilisation level corresponds to the range associated with acceptable delay performance.
Maximum staff hoursThe maximum number of operational duty hours that a controller may provide within a year.ControllableCompiled for each ANSP using RP4 Performance Plans [8], applicable national legislation, collective agreements and information collected through interviews. These sources are combined to reflect the operationally applicable limit in each ANSP.
SUPPLY
Current ATCOs in OPSThe number of ATCOs available at the start of the projection period, forming the baseline workforce from which future supply evolves.External or partially controllableDerived from the EUROCONTROL ACE benchmarking [1] dataset at ACC level, using the latest available number of ATCOs in service.
IntakeThe number of candidates entering the ATCO training pipeline each year. It determines the volume of controllers that can reach operations once training is complete.External or partially controllableObtained from the survey and interview programme, through which ANSPs reported current annual recruitment volumes and planned training intakes.
Training durationThe duration of the training sequence from entry into the pipeline to unit endorsement, comprising initial and unit training. It determines the delay between a recruitment decision and its effect on supply.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews. Training duration reflects the reported time required to progress from entry into the training pipeline to unit endorsement, including both initial and unit training phases.
Success rateThe proportion of trainees entering the training pipeline who successfully complete all training phases, obtain a unit endorsement and ultimately enter operational service. It determines the share of each intake that effectively contributes to future ATCO supply.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews, based on the proportion of trainees reported to successfully complete the training process and obtain a unit endorsement.
Training capacityThe number of trainee places the system can hold in each training phase, given the instructors, simulators and operating positions available. It caps the output of the pipeline, through the phase with the lowest throughput.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews. Capacity reflects the number of trainee places that can be supported by the available training resources, including instructors, simulators and operational training positions.
RetirementsControllers leaving operations on reaching the applicable retirement age. They reduce supply, at a volume determined by the age structure of the workforce.External or partially controllableWorkforce age profiles are derived from survey responses, while retirement ages are obtained from interviews and applicable national provisions. Both elements are combined to estimate future retirement flows.
AttritionAll other departures from operational duty, including resignation, movement to non-operational duties and loss of medical certification. They reduce supply, in addition to retirements.External or partially controllableBased on benchmark attrition rates reported in the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) Air Traffic Controller Workforce Plan. A common attrition rate is applied across the study area in the absence of equivalent European datasets.
