The ATCO pipeline

Intermediate Report · Chapter 2 · The ATCO management cycle

A shared process map links workforce planning, recruitment, training, licensing, deployment and feedback. It helps locate a challenge and trace its consequences across the management cycle.

The pipeline provides a common framework for comparing ANSP practices, considering regulatory constraints and identifying where decisions at one stage affect later outcomes.

The supply–demand model builds on a simplified representation of these processes to explore future workforce scenarios.

The supply–demand model

Final Report · §2.2 and Annex F · Model purpose and assumptions

An ACC-level model projects demand and supply to 2036 and aggregates results for ANSPs and Europe. Scenarios and sensitivity tests help users examine which factors most influence the gap.

The model supports workforce planning and explains how shortages emerge and evolve. It also allows the effects of operational, workforce and training measures on future controller availability to be compared.

Additionally, as with any forecasting exercise, the model incorporates a number of simplifying assumptions. In particular, demand and supply are assessed on an annual basis, workforce mobility between operational units is not modelled explicitly, and the analysis focuses on workforce availability rather than the economic implications of potential interventions.

The challenges and solutions catalogue

Final Report · Chapter 5 · From solutions to recommendations

The catalogue connects 55 challenges with 78 existing or potential practices. Records describe the challenges addressed, time horizon, implementation dependencies and affected model variables. The ANSP, governance and technology perspectives help identify the stakeholders and dependencies involved in implementation.

The 78 solutions were prioritised according to the importance of the challenges they address and the feasibility of implementation. The assessment criteria explain how these judgements were made.

Applying importance and feasibility criteria reduced 78 solutions to 36 candidate recommendations. Their assessments provide the starting point for the pillar and stakeholder shortlists.

The proposed competence and licensing framework

Final Report · §7.5 and §7.7 · Scope and further development

The CBL proposal distinguishes core, technical-system and operational-environment competence, supporting discussion of recognition, targeted training and future licensing.

Each competence domain comprises competencies built from knowledge, skills, and attitudes, which are explicitly defined and recognised separately, rather than relying on a single unit endorsement as the evidence for the entire bundle. The approach allows interaction between the competence domains but requires that all three domains are maintained, and that none can be completely replaced by competence in the other two domains. This notion is also widely supported by the interviews and stakeholder discussions.

The CBL concept offers a potential route to future-proofing training and licensing. Further work is needed to define its detailed procedures and requirements.
