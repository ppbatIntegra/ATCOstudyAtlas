Future-proofing the framework

Final Report · Chapter 7 · Making the framework future-proof

The workforce analysis identifies measures that mainly use the existing regulatory framework. The future-framework analysis then examines how changes in technology, operational concepts and service delivery affect training and licensing requirements.

What is future-proof?

Before we dive into the analyses, it is important to define what we mean by future-proof in this case. We understand that the European ATM Masterplan, its Strategic Deployment Objectives (SDOs), the Common Project 1 and the potential Common Project 2 regulations foresee operational changes which would significantly alter the ways ATM services are delivered, and, in the longer run, introduce a conceptual shift in how ATC is provided, in the form of Trajectory-based Operations (TBO). At the same time, the overall technological environment around air navigation services is also undergoing a rapid, increasingly fast-paced transformation, with the emergence of more advanced 3D visualisation tools, machine learning applications, artificial intelligence, and better connectivity. The deployment of existing technologies – let alone emerging ones – remains relatively slow in ATM compared to other industries due to a variety of reasons, creating a technological pressure from the “outside” world. This results in a situation where we already have good visibility of future ATM technologies, even though deployment is sometimes more than a decade away. While this situation is often quoted negatively, it offers clear benefits for our analysis, as it provides a window into the future, at least from a technical perspective.

It is reasonable to assume that the above technical and operational changes will affect how air traffic controllers perform their work and even affect what that work comprises. Technology, demographic, and social changes will also transform how people learn and how people are trained. Taken together, the ATCO training and licensing scheme will likely need to adapt to accommodate these changes.

Future-proofing the ATCO training and licensing scheme means acknowledging the need for change, assessing the required changes, and defining a framework flexible enough to accommodate them at appropriate levels, without revising the overall regulatory framework each time a new technology or concept is introduced, and ensuring that even technologies and concepts we do not yet know (despite our window into the future) will fit, to the extent practically possible.

While the above description of future-proofing sounds relatively straightforward, getting there is anything but. To make it manageable, we break it down into two phases:

  • First, we look at how the future for ATM and ATCOs in particular can be defined in more detail;
  • Second, we look at the proofing part and offer a solution we believe could be the way forward.

When defining the future for ATM and ATCOs, we examine new technologies and operational concepts, understand how they change ATCOs' jobs, and translate that shift into changes in the required skills, knowledge, and competences.

When looking at the proofing part, we seek a way forward that accommodates change while maintaining safety through clear requirements and demonstrable performance, preserving credible oversight via competent, evidence-based assurance, avoiding unnecessary administrative burden, and devising stable rules with adaptable details.
