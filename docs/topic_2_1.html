The ATCO gap

Final Report · §2.2, The ATCO gap

At the centre of the supply-demand model is the concept of the ATCO gap, defined as the difference between the number of controllers required to accommodate future traffic demand and the number of controllers expected to be available to provide ATS. Therefore, a positive gap indicates that workforce requirements exceed the number of available controllers, meaning an ATCO shortage. Conversely, a negative gap indicates that available workforce exceeds the estimated requirement.

The ATCO gap constitutes the principal output of the model and the main indicator used throughout the study to assess workforce sustainability. However, the gap should not be viewed as a static measure or simply as an estimate of future staffing shortages. Rather, it represents the cumulative effect of multiple hiring, training, licensing and operational factors interacting over time. As a result, understanding the causes of the gap is often more important than measuring its magnitude alone.

The time evolution of the ATCO gap is important because it reflects the effect of measures already applied in the present baseline year or any additional workforce shortages that may emerge during the projection period as traffic demand evolves and workforce dynamics change. When demand grows faster than supply, workforce shortages emerge, so the gap increases. Conversely, when supply grows faster than demand, the gap narrows and workforce pressure is reduced.

This time dimension is particularly relevant in ATM, where workforce planning is characterised by long lead times. Decisions regarding hiring, training, licensing and operational conditions may take several years to translate into additional ATCOs. Consequently, existing shortages typically reflect historical workforce planning decisions, recruitment practices, training constraints or operational conditions that have already materialised. Future shortages, by contrast, are the result of decisions and trends that are taking place today, but whose effects may only become visible several years later. The objective of the model is therefore not only to estimate whether a future workforce shortage will exist, but also to identify the mechanisms that create it and the factors most capable of reducing it. This analytical framework allows workforce challenges identified throughout the study to be linked to specific drivers of the gap and provides the basis for assessing the effectiveness of potential mitigation measures.
