Assessment of key areas

Final Report · §4.3, Assessment of key areas

Building on the prioritisation established in the previous section, this section examines each key area in detail to identify the specific challenges affecting ATCO availability and the measures capable of addressing them. The assessment follows a common structure across all pillars and key areas: the challenges reported by stakeholders through interviews and surveys are presented first, followed by the potential solutions. Each challenge and solution has a unique code, described in Annex H, which provides the corresponding detailed tables, so findings remain comparable across the lifecycle and can be linked to the model's quantitative framework.

Solutions are drawn from the consultation process, the literature review and the wider evidence collected throughout the study, and are grouped into two categories:

  • Best Practices (BP), corresponding to measures that are already being implemented by one or more ANSPs and for which practical evidence of application exists; and
  • Future Practices (FP), corresponding to measures, concepts or proposals that have been identified as potentially beneficial but for which no current implementation has been identified within the consultation sample.

Prioritisation

Final Report · Chapter 5, Summary of Solutions and Recommendations

Once the full set of potential measures was identified and defined, the 78 candidate solutions were prioritised against two criteria to isolate those best able to reduce the future ATCO gap: the importance of the challenges addressed and the feasibility of implementation. The detailed prioritisation criteria are presented in Annex I.

Applying these criteria reduced the initial set of 78 solutions to a shortlist of 36 candidate recommendations. The full assessment is attached in Annex J. These constitute the starting point for the recommendations presented in this section per pillar and are subsequently assessed from different stakeholder perspectives.

Strategic Planning (T-SP)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 7: Challenges reported in Strategic Planning (Source: Consortium assessment)
T-SP.1Traffic forecast volatility
Rationale
Traffic forecasts used to determine future staffing requirements are updated regularly, whereas the ATCO training pipeline typically requires several years to convert candidates to ATCOs. As a result, recruitment decisions are often based on demand assumptions that may change significantly before trainees enter operations.
ANSPs reporting
6 (35%)
T-SP.2Pressure to minimise staffing levels
Rationale
Cost-efficiency objectives and pressure to contain user charges can incentivise workforce planning approaches based on minimum staffing levels. This reduces the margin available to absorb forecast uncertainty, traffic surges or unforeseen operational requirements.
ANSPs reporting
6 (35%)
T-SP.3Delayed consequences of workforce planning decisions
Rationale
The length of the ATCO training pipeline means that deficiencies in recruitment and workforce planning may remain invisible for several years before materialising as operational staffing shortages. Consequently, corrective measures often become effective only after significant delays.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
T-SP.4Annual approval of workforce positions
Rationale
In some ANSPs, operational positions require periodic approval through government or budgetary processes. This creates uncertainty regarding the long-term availability of posts and complicates multi-year workforce and training planning.
ANSPs reporting
3 (18%)

Working Conditions (H-WC)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 9: Challenges reported in Working Conditions (Source: Consortium assessment)
H-WC.1Uncertain retirement behaviour
Rationale
In environments where controllers are not subject to a fixed retirement age, workforce departures depend largely on individual decisions. This reduces planning certainty and complicates the forecasting of future replacement requirements, especially if the notice time requirements are also short.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-WC.2Compensation disparities between workforce cohorts
Rationale
Differences in remuneration between newer and more experienced controllers performing similar roles may create perceptions of inequity, affecting workforce morale, engagement and retention.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-WC.3Loss of controllers to more attractive labour markets
Rationale
Qualified controllers may leave for employers, regions or sectors offering more attractive compensation packages or career opportunities, reducing the retention of experienced operational staff.
ANSPs reporting
3 (18%)
H-WC.4Early retirements associated with major operational changes
Rationale
The introduction of major operational systems or technologies may encourage some experienced controllers to retire earlier than anticipated rather than adapt to new operating environments, affecting workforce planning assumptions.
ANSPs reporting
2 (12%)
H-WC.5Cost-of-living pressures affecting workforce retention
Rationale
In locations with particularly high living costs, newly qualified controllers may experience difficulties remaining close to their assigned unit, increasing the likelihood of early workforce departures.
ANSPs reporting
1 (6%)
H-WC.6Limited career development and workforce flexibility
Rationale
A lack of visible career progression opportunities and flexible working arrangements may reduce long-term workforce engagement and negatively affect retention.
ANSPs reporting
1 (6%)

Recruitment (H-R)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 11: Challenges reported in Recruitment (Source: Consortium assessment)
H-R.1Restrictions on age-based recruitment criteria
Rationale
Legal limitations on the use of age-based recruitment criteria reduce the ability of some ANSPs to tailor recruitment strategies to long-term workforce planning considerations.
ANSPs reporting
7 (41%)
H-R.2Limited availability of suitable candidates
Rationale
Recruitment campaigns may attract large numbers of applicants, but only a relatively small proportion possess the aptitude and characteristics required to succeed in ATCO training and operations. As a result, workforce constraints are often driven by the quality rather than the quantity of applications received.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-R.3Restrictive medical requirements
Rationale
Medical certification requirements may exclude otherwise suitable candidates from the recruitment pipeline, reducing the pool of applicants capable of progressing into training.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-R.4Limited awareness of the profession
Rationale
The ATCO profession and, in some cases, individual ANSPs may lack visibility among potential applicants, limiting the size of the candidate pool entering recruitment processes.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-R.5Financial barriers to entry into training
Rationale
Training arrangements involving limited remuneration or prolonged periods without income may discourage otherwise suitable candidates from pursuing the profession.
ANSPs reporting
2 (12%)

Selection (H-S)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 13: Challenges reported in Selection (Source: Consortium assessment)
H-S.1Difficulty assessing non-technical competencies
Rationale
Competencies such as trainability, teamwork, communication, resilience and behaviour under pressure are difficult to assess consistently and may not be adequately captured by existing selection methods.
ANSPs reporting
8 (47%)
H-S.2Limited predictive accuracy of selection systems
Rationale
Existing selection methods do not always reliably predict which candidates will successfully complete ATCO training and progress to operational endorsement. In some cases, assessment results show only a limited correlation with subsequent training performance.
ANSPs reporting
8 (47%)
H-S.3Limited feedback between training outcomes and selection criteria
Rationale
Information on training success and failure is not systematically used to refine selection methodologies, reducing the ability of organisations to continuously improve candidate assessment processes.
ANSPs reporting
7 (41%)
H-S.4Medical assessment constraints
Rationale
Medical examinations may introduce delays, additional costs and administrative complexity due to limited availability of certified aeromedical assessors and differences in the application of medical requirements across jurisdictions.
ANSPs reporting
7 (41%)
H-S.5Lengthy selection processes
Rationale
Selection procedures may extend over several months, increasing the risk of losing suitable candidates to alternative employment opportunities before they enter training.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
H-S.6Reduced validity due to test preparation and coaching
Rationale
The growing availability of preparation material for selection tests may undermine their ability to distinguish genuine aptitude from familiarity with the assessment process.
ANSPs reporting
4 (24%)

Initial Training (TL-IT)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 15: Challenges reported in Initial training (Source: Consortium assessment)
TL-IT.1Lack of consensus on generic versus unit-specific training
Rationale
ANSPs differ in their views regarding whether initial training should be delivered using generic operational environments or tailored to specific units. While generic training may be easier to standardise and scale, unit-specific training may better prepare trainees for subsequent operational phases.
ANSPs reporting
9 (53%)
TL-IT.2Limited opportunities for independent practical training
Rationale
Trainees often require access to supervised simulators and instructors in order to practise, limiting the amount of training that can be undertaken independently and constraining overall training throughput.
ANSPs reporting
6 (35%)
TL-IT.3Limited interoperability between training systems
Rationale
Differences in simulator platforms, training systems and exercise design reduce opportunities for sharing training material and resources between ANSPs, resulting in duplication of effort across the network.
ANSPs reporting
4 (24%)
TL-IT.4Lengthy initial training programmes
Rationale
Several stakeholders consider that elements of the initial training process could potentially be streamlined or reorganised, although any reduction in duration must preserve the competencies required for safe operational performance.
ANSPs reporting
4 (24%)
TL-IT.5Difficulties in expanding simulator capacity
Rationale
The acquisition and deployment of additional simulator resources can require lengthy procurement and implementation processes, limiting the ability of training organisations to respond rapidly to increased demand.
ANSPs reporting
3 (18%)
TL-IT.6Limitations of simulator-based competency assessment
Rationale
Certain operational competencies remain difficult to replicate or assess consistently within simulated environments, limiting the extent to which simulator performance alone can be used as a measure of operational readiness.
ANSPs reporting
3 (18%)

Unit Training (TL-UT)

Challenges

Final Report · §4.3 · Table 17: Challenges reported in Unit training (Source: Consortium assessment)
TL-UT.1Limited availability of qualified instructors
Rationale
Unit training requires significant involvement from qualified instructors and assessors. Limited instructor availability directly constrains the number of trainees that can progress through the training pipeline.
ANSPs reporting
7 (41%)
TL-UT.2Training activity displaced by operational demand
Rationale
During periods of high traffic demand, instructors are frequently required to prioritise operational duties, resulting in delays or interruptions to training activities.
ANSPs reporting
6 (35%)
TL-UT.3Training capacity constraints limiting workforce expansion
Rationale
Increasing recruitment does not automatically increase the number of qualified controllers if the unit training system lacks sufficient capacity to absorb additional trainees. In such circumstances, trainees accumulate within the pipeline without increasing operational output.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
TL-UT.4Limited flexibility within training and validation requirements
Rationale
Fixed training-hour requirements and rigid validation processes reduce the ability of ANSPs to adapt training programmes to changing operational circumstances and trainee performance.
ANSPs reporting
5 (29%)
TL-UT.5Competition between training and operational capacity
Rationale
On-the-job training requires instructors to dedicate operational time to training activities. As a result, training throughput is inherently linked to the availability of operational resources.
ANSPs reporting
4 (24%)
