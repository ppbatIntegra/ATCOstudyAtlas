From solutions to recommendations

Final Report · Chapter 5 · Solutions and recommendations

The 78 solutions were prioritised according to the importance of the challenges they address and the feasibility of implementation. The assessment criteria explain how these judgements were made.

Applying importance and feasibility criteria reduced 78 solutions to 36 candidate recommendations. Their assessments provide the starting point for the pillar and stakeholder shortlists.

To facilitate assessment, we first organised the 36 candidate recommendations according to the four pillars of the ATCO pipeline used throughout the study: Transversal, Hiring, Training & Licensing, and Operational. The complete list of candidate recommendations, together with the challenges addressed, implementation horizon, degree of control and affected model variables, is provided in candidate recommendations by pillar.

The following subsections describe the rationale for prioritising solutions within each pillar and present the recommendations ultimately retained. This approach ensures consistency with the analytical framework applied throughout the study whilst allowing the most relevant measures to be identified within each area of the ATCO management cycle.

Importance, feasibility and prioritisation

Final Report · Annex I · Assessment criteria

In order to have a common assessment framework through which all identified solutions can be evaluated consistently and compared across key areas, these have been analysed on two complementary criteria:

  • Importance, which measures the relevance of a solution in terms of the challenges it addresses and the number of ANSPs affected by those challenges; and
  • Feasibility, which measures the relative ease with which a solution can be implemented, taking into account both the expected implementation time horizon and the degree of control over its implementation from the ANSP point of view.

The Importance of each solution is calculated using a scoring approach based on the challenges addressed by the solution. Specifically, the score corresponds to the sum of the ANSPs reporting each of the challenges that the solution seeks to address, according to its definition and considering the information from the interviews conducted and the survey responses.

Consequently, solutions targeting challenges reported by a larger number of ANSPs receive higher scores, reflecting their broader relevance across the European ATM network.

The resulting score is subsequently translated into a qualitative classification of High, Medium or Low Importance according to the criteria presented below.

While Importance assesses the relevance of a solution, Feasibility assesses the extent to which it can realistically be implemented. The criterion combines two complementary dimensions: the implementation horizon, which reflects the period required for the effects of a solution to become visible, and the level of control over implementation, which reflects the degree to which implementation depends on the ANSP itself or on external actors.

The implementation time horizon is classified as:

  • Short-term, where tangible effects are expected within one to two years;
  • Medium-term, where effects are expected within three to five years; and
  • Long-term, where the effects of implementation are expected to materialise after more than five years.

The level of control is classified according to the degree of dependency on external actors:

  • High control, where implementation depends exclusively on actions undertaken by the ANSP;
  • Medium control, where implementation additionally depends on the availability, maturity or deployment of supporting technologies; and
  • Low control, where implementation requires regulatory, legislative or organisational action by external stakeholders, either at Member State or European level.

These two dimensions are subsequently combined to derive an overall Feasibility rating, following the assessment matrix presented below.

The prioritisation matrix places primary emphasis on the Importance of each solution, as determined by the relevance and prevalence of the challenges it addresses across participating ANSPs. Solutions classified as Low Importance are not considered further, while those classified as High Importance are automatically retained as candidate recommendations. The assessment of Feasibility is therefore only relevant for solutions with Medium Importance.

The rationale behind this approach is to ensure that potentially impactful measures are not disregarded solely because their implementation requires long-term actions, regulatory amendments or the involvement of external stakeholders. As a result, the prioritisation framework favours strategic relevance and potential impact on ATCO shortages, while still taking implementation considerations into account where appropriate.
