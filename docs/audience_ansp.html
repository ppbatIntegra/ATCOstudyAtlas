ANSP perspective

Final Report · §5.2, ANSP

In the previous sections, we have effectively filtered and prioritised the solutions from the ANSP perspective. The initial set of 78 solutions was reduced to 36 candidate recommendations through the application of the importance and feasibility criteria described previously. This analysis assesses these 36 candidate recommendations collectively, regardless of pillar, to identify the measures with the greatest overall potential to reduce future ATCO shortages from an ANSP perspective.

To do so, the candidate recommendations are first filtered based on the sensitivity analysis results. Priority is given to solutions affecting ATCO productivity and/or utilisation, as these variables were identified as the most influential controllable drivers of the ATCO gap. Among these, we prefer solutions that affect more than one model variable, as they can generate greater overall impact. This leaves four candidate recommendations. One of these additionally depends on enabling technologies and was therefore considered less feasible than the remaining measures.

Table 36: Global top three — ANSP (operational) perspective (Source: Consortium assessment)

IDName
TL-UT.BP.1Dedicated management of instructor roles and workloads
TL-UT.FP.2Adopt competency-based progression models
TL-L.BP.2Facilitate mobility through common operational language arrangements and digital licence management

The resulting top three recommendations all belong to the Training & Licensing pillar and correspond to the same measures previously identified as the highest-priority recommendations within that pillar. This outcome reinforces the conclusions of the preceding analysis: the greatest opportunity to improve future controller availability lies not in increasing recruitment alone, but in strengthening the efficiency, capacity and flexibility of the training and licensing pipeline.

The result is also fully consistent with the preliminary qualitative assessment and the root-cause analysis. From the ANSP perspective, Unit Training emerged as the most critical area, while training capacity, training throughput, and workforce flexibility were repeatedly identified as the principal constraints affecting future controller availability. The prioritisation process therefore confirms that measures targeting these issues offer the greatest potential to reduce the future ATCO gap and strengthen the long-term resilience of the European ATCO workforce system.

Workforce planning and operational measures

Final Report · §5.1, Transversal and Operational

Within the Transversal pillar, only one recommendation is retained. All candidate solutions identified for this pillar act on the Intake variable. However, one solution also affects Training Capacity, enabling a greater proportion of future recruitment activity to translate into ATCOs, so it has the greatest potential impact on the future ATCO gap and is therefore retained as the recommended measure for this pillar.

Table 32: Recommendation — Transversal pillar (Source: Consortium assessment)

IDRecommended solution
T-SP.FP.2Integrate traffic and training-capacity scenarios into workforce planning

The retained recommendation reflects the conclusion that workforce planning should not focus solely on how many controllers to recruit, but also on whether the training system can handle the load. By simultaneously addressing intake and training capacity, the selected measure provides a more robust response to future workforce needs and better aligns workforce planning decisions with the realities of the ATCO training pipeline.

Guided by the sensitivity analysis, we prioritised solutions that affect Utilisation and ATCO Productivity, the two operational variables with the greatest influence on the projected ATCO gap. Almost all candidate measures within the Operational pillar affect one or both of these variables and were therefore considered relevant for further assessment.

The selection was subsequently refined based on the breadth of challenges addressed and the feasibility of implementation. The only candidate acting exclusively on Training Duration was discarded, as the sensitivity analysis demonstrated that changes in this variable alone do not directly affect the future ATCO gap. Priority was then given to measures addressing a wider range of challenges. Consequently, we retained the measure addressing three operational challenges (OR.BP.3), while excluding those addressing a single challenge.

Among the remaining candidates, we discarded two solutions because they relied more heavily on future technological developments and carried greater implementation uncertainty. This process yielded the three recommendations presented below, which combine strong potential impact on the ATCO gap with a comparatively higher degree of practical feasibility.

Table 35: Recommendations — Operational pillar (Source: Consortium assessment)

IDName
O-R.BP.3Reduce structural dependence on overtime through roster redesign
O-O.BP.5Develop competencies for automation-enabled operational environments
O-O.FP.2Expand implementation of advanced airspace management concepts

The recommendations retained within the Operational pillar reflect the importance of improving workforce utilisation and operational productivity rather than increasing the number of controllers entering the system. While the training-related recommendations focus on strengthening controller supply, the operational recommendations aim to maximise capacity from the existing workforce.

This prioritisation is consistent with the sensitivity analysis, which identified ATCO productivity and utilisation as the variables with the greatest influence on the projected ATCO gap. The selected measures therefore focus on reducing structural dependence on overtime, preparing controllers for increasingly automated operating environments and supporting the deployment of advanced operational concepts. Together, these recommendations aim to increase operational capacity and workforce flexibility, thereby mitigating future shortages from the demand side of the supply-demand model.
