Final Report model

Final Report · Annex F, Detailed specification of the supply-demand model

This annex provides the detailed technical specification of the supply-demand model developed for the study. It documents the calculation logic used to compute the two components of the ATCO gap formula: ATCO demand and ATCO supply, detailing the assumptions considered during the development of the model and including the definition of all the variables used to do so, together with the corresponding sources.

The model is developed at ACC level and subsequently aggregated to ANSP and European level. It projects annual workforce developments from the baseline year to 2036 and relies on the best available combination of harmonised European datasets and ANSP-specific information obtained through surveys and interviews. Figure 18 illustrates the geographical coverage of the study and the availability of ANSP-specific information collected through the consultation activities. Whilst responses were obtained from the majority of ANSPs within the study scope, a limited number of ANSPs did not participate in the consultation, requiring the use of publicly available information and supplementary assumptions for the parameterisation of certain model variables.

Additionally, as with any forecasting exercise, the model incorporates a number of simplifying assumptions. In particular, demand and supply are assessed on an annual basis, workforce mobility between operational units is not modelled explicitly, and the analysis focuses on workforce availability rather than the economic implications of potential interventions.

The supply-demand model is parameterised using a defined set of variables that determine the evolution of both ATCO demand and ATCO supply over the projection period.

For transparency and traceability, the following summarises the variables incorporated into the model, their definitions, degree of controllability and the principal data sources used for their parameterisation.

Flight controlled hoursThe volume of Instrumental Flight Rules (IFR) traffic handled by an ACC, expressed as flight hours controlled. The selected utilisation level corresponds to the range associated with acceptable delay performance.

IntakeThe number of candidates entering the ATCO training pipeline each year. It determines the volume of controllers that can reach operations once training is complete.External or partially controllableObtained from the survey and interview programme, through which ANSPs reported current annual recruitment volumes and planned training intakes.
Training durationThe duration of the training sequence from entry into the pipeline to unit endorsement, comprising initial and unit training. It determines the delay between a recruitment decision and its effect on supply.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews. Training duration reflects the reported time required to progress from entry into the training pipeline to unit endorsement, including both initial and unit training phases.
Success rateThe proportion of trainees entering the training pipeline who successfully complete all training phases, obtain a unit endorsement and ultimately enter operational service. It determines the share of each intake that effectively contributes to future ATCO supply.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews, based on the proportion of trainees reported to successfully complete the training process and obtain a unit endorsement.
Training capacityThe number of trainee places the system can hold in each training phase, given the instructors, simulators and operating positions available. It caps the output of the pipeline, through the phase with the lowest throughput.ControllableObtained from survey responses and interviews. Capacity reflects the number of trainee places that can be supported by the available training resources, including instructors, simulators and operational training positions.
RetirementsControllers leaving operations on reaching the applicable retirement age. They reduce supply, at a volume determined by the age structure of the workforce.External or partially controllableWorkforce age profiles are derived from survey responses, while retirement ages are obtained from interviews and applicable national provisions. Both elements are combined to estimate future retirement flows.
AttritionAll other departures from operational duty, including resignation, movement to non-operational duties and loss of medical certification. They reduce supply, in addition to retirements.External or partially controllableBased on benchmark attrition rates reported in the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) Air Traffic Controller Workforce Plan. A common attrition rate is applied across the study area in the absence of equivalent European datasets.
