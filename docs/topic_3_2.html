Assessment of key areas

Final Report · §4.3, Assessment of key areas

Building on the prioritisation established in the previous section, this section examines each key area in detail to identify the specific challenges affecting ATCO availability and the measures capable of addressing them. The assessment follows a common structure across all pillars and key areas: the challenges reported by stakeholders through interviews and surveys are presented first, followed by the potential solutions. Each challenge and solution has a unique code, described in Annex H, which provides the corresponding detailed tables, so findings remain comparable across the lifecycle and can be linked to the model's quantitative framework.

Solutions are drawn from the consultation process, the literature review and the wider evidence collected throughout the study, and are grouped into two categories:

  • Best Practices (BP), corresponding to measures that are already being implemented by one or more ANSPs and for which practical evidence of application exists; and
  • Future Practices (FP), corresponding to measures, concepts or proposals that have been identified as potentially beneficial but for which no current implementation has been identified within the consultation sample.

Selection and unit training

The challenges identified within Selection fall into two closely related categories: improving the ability of selection systems to identify future successful controllers and improving the efficiency of the selection process itself.

The most consistently reported concerns relate to the predictive capability of current selection methodologies. Several ANSPs highlighted that existing tools do not always provide a reliable indication of future training performance, particularly when assessing non-technical competencies such as communication, teamwork, trainability and performance under pressure. Stakeholders also noted that selection systems frequently operate as isolated processes, with limited feedback from subsequent training outcomes to inform future improvements.

At the same time, a number of ANSPs identified operational challenges affecting the efficiency of the selection pipeline. Medical assessments were frequently reported as a source of delay and inconsistency, while lengthy recruitment and selection procedures increase the risk of losing strong candidates before training begins. Concerns regarding coaching and test preparation further reinforce the perception that current selection systems may not always provide the most accurate representation of candidate suitability.

Taken together, these findings suggest that improvements in Selection are likely to derive not only from more rigorous assessment methods, but also from stronger integration between recruitment, selection, training and operational performance data. As Selection directly influences the characteristics of individuals entering the training pipeline, weaknesses at this stage have the potential to affect all subsequent stages of the ATCO management cycle.

The challenges identified within Unit Training are primarily associated with capacity constraints and the inherent difficulty of scaling training activities within operational environments. Unlike earlier stages of the training pipeline, where capacity can be increased through additional classroom or simulator resources, Unit Training depends directly on operational positions, instructors and live traffic environments that must simultaneously support both training and operational service delivery.

The challenges identified within Unit Training confirm the results of the key area assessment presented in Section 4.2 and explain why this area emerges as the principal bottleneck affecting future ATCO availability. Unlike recruitment or selection, where constraints primarily affect the number of candidates entering the pipeline, Unit Training directly determines how many trainees ultimately become ATCOs.

The most significant challenges relate to instructor availability and the competition between training and operational activities. Stakeholders repeatedly emphasised that training capacity, rather than trainee availability, constitutes the primary constraint on workforce growth. Several ANSPs highlighted that increasing recruitment without simultaneously expanding unit training capacity simply creates larger trainee backlogs rather than producing additional controllers.

The consultation also revealed the structural nature of many of these constraints. Instructor resources are limited, training activities are frequently suspended during periods of operational pressure, and the one-to-one nature of OJT makes it difficult to increase throughput without consuming operational capacity. At the same time, rigid training and validation arrangements may reduce the flexibility available to ANSPs when attempting to adapt training activity to changing operational conditions.

Taken together, these findings demonstrate that workforce shortages cannot be addressed through recruitment measures alone. The ability of the training system to convert trainees into ATCOs ultimately depends on the capacity, efficiency and flexibility of Unit Training, making it one of the most influential stages of the ATCO management cycle.

Retrospective root causes

Intermediate Report · Chapter 3, Summary

The key finding – so far – from the analysis is that there is no dominant reason behind ATCO shortages in Europe: The top five contributors to ATCO shortage-related delays all reported different reasons, and also display different data. However, there are a few emerging commonalities.

Firstly, three ANSPs noted limitations in rostering schemes as a key reason for staff shortages. In France and Spain, this was partly due to the rigidity of national legislation specifying rostering parameters. In contrast, in Hungary, rostering is set by the ANSP (but is also subject to collective bargaining agreements).

Secondly, unexpected traffic shifts and growth, and the uneven distribution of traffic, were also mentioned as key reasons. It is yet unclear whether this indicates problems with planning and forecasting or the growing uncertainty in air transport.

All ANSPs mentioned the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and how restrictions and social distancing requirements were detrimental to ATCO training. However, in some cases, the ATCO numbers we observe in the datasets do not clearly confirm these issues.

Finally, an important emerging finding is that where the central administration of the state and/or the government imposes restrictions on the operation of the ANSP (either in the form of specifying operational procedures and parameters in national legislation or requiring government/ministerial approval for operational decisions), ATCO shortages and capacity problems appear to be more likely to occur. This may be due to the reduced ability to react to changes in the operational environment.

As for the study’s focus topic, none of the interviews indicated that European regulations constrained the training and licensing processes or served as a driver or reason for the issues.
