The challenge across the lifecycle

Final Report · Introduction

The European Air Traffic Management (ATM) network has experienced recurring Air Traffic Controller (ATCO) shortages for more than a decade, making workforce availability one of the most persistent challenges facing the system. ATCO availability is influenced by a wide range of interrelated factors acting across the ATCO management cycle, from workforce planning and recruitment through training, licensing and operational deployment. This study systematically identified these factors and structured them into pillars and key areas, providing the foundation for the subsequent assessment of challenges and potential solutions.

While the issue’s severity and geographical distribution have varied over time, its operational consequences have become increasingly visible across the network. Prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, traffic demand grew more rapidly than the ATCO workforce in many parts of Europe, contributing to increasing levels of Air Traffic Control (ATC) capacity-related Air Traffic Flow Management (ATFM) delay. Although the pandemic temporarily reduced traffic demand and alleviated workforce pressures, the subsequent recovery exposed persistent structural weaknesses in the controller workforce pipeline. As traffic demand returned to, and in several cases exceeded, pre-pandemic levels, many Air Navigation Service Providers (ANSPs) were unable to increase controller availability at a comparable pace.

The resulting imbalance between demand for air traffic services and available workforce capacity has become one of the principal constraints affecting network performance. The analysis undertaken as part of this study quantifies this imbalance and estimates its future evolution based on workforce dynamics and operational trends observed across the European ATM network.

The analysis in the Intermediate Report, including the literature review from the previous phase of the study, showed that these shortages cannot be attributed to a single operational event or isolated workforce issue. Instead, multiple long-term factors are acting simultaneously, including extended training lead times, retirement pressures, changing traffic patterns, workforce planning challenges, and the system's limited ability to increase controller availability within short timeframes. As a result, workforce shortages have evolved from a localised resource challenge into a structural issue affecting the resilience and performance of the European ATM network.

Prioritising the key areas

Final Report · §4.2 · Prioritisation of key areas

The root-cause analysis focuses on the areas of the ATCO management cycle most likely to influence future controller availability. Whilst all key areas contribute to the future balance between ATCO demand and supply, their relative importance differs considerably. To identify the areas requiring the most attention, we assess evidence from interviews and surveys using two complementary indicators: Incidence and Severity. The results show a clear concentration of challenges in a limited number of areas of the ATCO management cycle. Unit Training emerges as the most critical key area, being the only one classified as High in both Incidence and Severity. Stakeholders consistently identified instructor availability, on-the-job training capacity and training throughput as the principal constraints affecting the production of new controllers.

This finding is particularly significant because Unit Training represents the final stage of the training process before a trainee is validated and becomes operational. Consequently, constraints affecting this stage have a direct impact on the number of ATCOs available to provide services and limit the ability of ANSPs to convert recruitment efforts into operational capacity. The prominence of Unit Training across both interviews and survey responses suggests that workforce shortages are driven less by candidate availability and more by the system's capacity to qualify them efficiently.

A second group of key areas also warrants particular attention. Selection and Operations present High Severity scores, indicating that their associated challenges are experienced by a large proportion of ANSPs across the network. Meanwhile, Recruitment, Sector-Specific Training and Licensing show High Incidence scores, demonstrating their prominence within stakeholder discussions and their perceived importance for workforce flexibility, mobility and long-term controller availability.

Taken together, these findings suggest that the most significant workforce constraints are concentrated in the later stages of the ATCO management cycle, particularly those linked to training throughput, operational deployment and workforce flexibility. They also indicate that addressing ATCO shortages requires a broader perspective than recruitment alone. While attracting candidates remains important, the evidence suggests that the ability to train, qualify, deploy and retain ATCOs efficiently is a more significant determinant of future workforce availability.

By contrast, areas such as ANSP Factors, Exogenous Factors (of Strategic Planning), Initial Training, and Post-Ops Analysis were mentioned less often and generally viewed as less direct contributors to workforce shortages. Although these areas influence workforce performance and may enable or constrain it, stakeholders rarely identified them as the primary source of current workforce pressures. As a result, they are considered secondary priorities within the overall root-cause analysis.
