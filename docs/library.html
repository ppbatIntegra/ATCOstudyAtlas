The study’s evidence and outputs

Final Report · Executive Summary

Building on this framework, the study combines quantitative and qualitative evidence to assess both the scale of the workforce challenge and its underlying causes. The quantitative analysis measures the current and projected ATCO gap and evaluates the sensitivity of workforce availability to changes in key variables. The qualitative analysis complements these results by providing operational context, identifying root causes and assessing the practical feasibility of potential solutions.

The evidence combines public baseline datasets, a survey completed by 26 ANSPs and in-depth interviews with 17 ANSPs. Together these inform the model and the assessment of workforce, training and licensing constraints.

To identify the most effective responses, the study assessed both existing practices implemented by European ANSPs and future opportunities identified through literature review and stakeholder consultation. In total, 78 solutions were mapped against the workforce challenges they address and the variables influencing the ATCO gap. These solutions were subsequently prioritised according to their importance and implementation feasibility, before being refined through the sensitivity analysis of the supply-demand model. This process reduced the initial longlist to 36 candidate recommendations, representing the measures with the strongest combination of potential impact and practical deliverability.

Automation, new ATM systems, cross-border operations, and evolving service-delivery models increasingly change what controllers do and how responsibilities are shared between humans and systems. The current training and licensing framework relies on ratings and unit endorsements that bundle together a wide range of capabilities. This limits transparency on which competencies are transferable and can make mobility and requalification more burdensome than necessary.

The study addresses this by developing a task- and competency-based analytical framework for the ATCO role. It examines how automation changes individual ATCO tasks, separates aptitude, knowledge, skills and attitudes, competence, demonstrated performance and formal qualification, and identifies which elements of competence are generic and which depend on a specific technical or operational environment. A key contribution of the study is the resulting three-domain model of core ATCO competencies, technical-system competence and operational-environment competence, providing a structured basis for analysing transferability, training needs and qualification gaps.

Building on this analysis, the study develops a competency-based licensing (CBL) concept in which qualification focuses more directly on what an ATCO can demonstrably do, in which technical environment and under which operational conditions. Rather than treating a change of unit or environment as requiring a largely complete requalification pathway, the concept enables the recognition of already demonstrated competencies and focuses training and assessment on the remaining gaps. This provides a basis for more modular, targeted training; greater mobility and cross-border deployment; better recognition of prior competence; and a licensing framework that can evolve with technology and automation while maintaining the required level of operational safety.
