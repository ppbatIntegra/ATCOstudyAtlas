Study methodology

Final Report · Chapter 2, Methodology

To provide a comprehensive assessment of the European ATCO workforce situation, the study combines quantitative and qualitative sources of evidence within a single analytical framework. The quantitative component is used to assess the current and future balance between ATCO demand and supply and to estimate the magnitude of potential workforce gaps under different assumptions. The qualitative component is used to identify and assess the challenges, practices and constraints affecting workforce availability across the ATCO management cycle.

The methodology is structured around a sequence of interconnected analytical activities, illustrated in the following figure. The assessment begins with data collection and stakeholder consultation activities, which provide the evidence required to support both the quantitative and qualitative analyses. These analyses are subsequently integrated to identify the factors exerting the greatest influence on workforce availability, assess potential improvement measures and develop evidence-based recommendations for the future evolution of the European ATCO licensing framework and workforce management practices.

The quantitative assessment combines publicly available European datasets, official publications and information obtained directly from ANSPs to establish a common understanding of the current workforce situation and develop a European ATCO supply-demand model. The model is used to estimate the future relationship between workforce availability and operational requirements up to 2036 and to assess how different workforce, training and operational variables influence the projected workforce gap.

The qualitative assessment draws on stakeholder surveys, interviews and targeted consultations conducted across the European ATM network. This evidence is structured through an ATCO management cycle that enables challenges, constraints and potential solutions to be analysed consistently across different stages of the workforce system. Particular attention is given to understanding the factors influencing the variables identified through the quantitative assessment and to capturing operational and organisational perspectives that cannot be derived from quantitative data alone.

The integration of both components represents a central feature of the study. The quantitative analysis identifies the scale of the workforce challenge and the variables driving future workforce availability, whilst the qualitative analysis explains why these variables behave as they do and identifies the measures most capable of influencing them. Together, they provide the evidence base used throughout the remainder of this report.
