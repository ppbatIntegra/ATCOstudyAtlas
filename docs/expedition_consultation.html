Interview methodology

Final Report · §2.1 · Interviews

Interviews were conducted with ANSPs across the study scope to obtain qualitative evidence on the factors influencing ATCO availability that cannot be derived from publicly available datasets alone. The objective was to capture operational, organisational, training and regulatory perspectives relating to the management of the ATCO workforce and to provide context for the trends identified through the quantitative assessment.

Semi-structured interviews were conducted using a common interview guide to ensure consistency across participating organisations. The interviews were carried out through a combination of in-person and online meetings, depending on the circumstances and availability of each ANSP. The consultation covered workforce planning, recruitment, selection, training, licensing, operational deployment, workforce flexibility and future developments affecting the ATCO management cycle.

The interview programme included 17 of the 30 ANSPs within the scope of the study, representing approximately 57% of the target population. Whilst the participation rate does not cover the entire study scope, the sample was selected and completed in a way that ensures representation across the principal types of ANSPs operating within the SES. In particular, the participating organisations collectively cover the full range of:

  • ANSP sizes;
  • Geographical contexts;
  • Traffic profiles, in terms of the traffic seasonability;
  • Civil-military integration models;
  • Operational performance contexts, regarding the ATCO shortage;
  • Ownership and governance structures;
  • Market environments, covering both fully regulated and partially liberalised contexts;
  • Employment frameworks;
  • Training delivery models; and
  • ATM system and technology environments, including the principal technical alliances and platform families currently deployed across Europe.

The interview sample covers a broad range of organisational, operational and regulatory environments. Its findings inform the aggregate qualitative assessment of workforce challenges.

Survey methodology

Final Report · §2.1 · Survey

A dedicated survey was distributed to ANSPs across the study scope to collect standardised ANSP-specific information that is not consistently available through publicly accessible sources. The survey complemented the baseline datasets and interview programme by gathering information on workforce planning, recruitment, selection, training, licensing, workforce flexibility and other factors influencing ATCO availability across the ATCO management cycle.

The consortium developed the questionnaire around the study’s information needs. The European Commission and Project Steering Group reviewed and validated it. The questionnaire and aggregate responses are presented in the consultation material.

The survey remained open until 31 July 2026. Responses were received from 26 of the 30 ANSPs within the study scope, giving a participation rate exceeding 85% and broad coverage of the European ATM network.

Consultation coverage

Final Report · Chapter 3 · Consultation coverage

To this end, the consortium collected ANSPs direct information through 17 interviews and 26 survey responses from participating ANSPs. Together, these consultation activities provided direct insight into the different stages of the ATCO management cycle and complemented the quantitative evidence used in the supply-demand model.

The consultation covered a wide range of operational, organisational and regulatory environments across the SES area.

Aggregate findings

Final Report · §3.1 · Aggregate interview analysis

The 17-ANSP interview programme added organisational, operational and regulatory context to the quantitative evidence. Participating organisations covered diverse operational environments, ownership models and geographical settings.

The aggregate findings identify common concerns and differences in hiring, training, licensing and operational deployment.

Despite the diversity of operational environments represented in the study, several themes emerged consistently across the interviews. These common topics reflect shared challenges and concerns affecting the future availability of controllers throughout the European ATM network:

  • The ATCO shortage is a pipeline challenge: Interviewees consistently described workforce availability as the outcome of a long and interconnected pipeline extending from workforce planning and recruitment through training, licensing and operational deployment.
  • Long-term workforce planning is essential: Most ANSPs highlighted the difficulty of aligning recruitment and training decisions with future operational needs given the multi-year delay between recruitment and operational deployment.
  • Unit training is the main bottleneck: Whilst training arrangements vary across organisations, many ANSPs identified instructor availability, On-The-Job Training (OJT) capacity and unit training throughput as the primary constraints limiting workforce growth.
  • Selection quality is as important as recruitment volume: Interviewees frequently emphasised the importance of selecting candidates with the behavioural, interpersonal and learning attributes required to successfully complete training and perform operational duties.
  • Technology is expected to influence future workforce requirements: Most ANSPs anticipate that automation, digitalisation and future operational concepts will affect the way controllers are trained and deployed, although there is less consensus regarding the scale and timing of these changes.

Alongside these common themes, the interviews revealed important differences in how ANSPs manage their workforce pipelines. These differences often reflect national governance arrangements, organisational models and operational environments:

  • Governance and workforce planning arrangements vary significantly: Some ANSPs have substantial autonomy over recruitment and staffing decisions, while others operate within public-sector frameworks requiring external approval for workforce planning changes.
  • Training arrangements differ considerably between organisations: ANSPs vary in the extent to which training is delivered internally or externally, as well as in training duration, endorsement structures and operational specialisation requirements.
  • Views on controller mobility remain mixed: While greater mobility is generally viewed positively, many ANSPs highlighted that operational competence remains strongly linked to unit-specific training, local procedures, airspace characteristics and language requirements in case of cross-border mobility.
  • Different approaches exist regarding workforce utilisation and working arrangements: ANSPs reported varying practices relating to rostering, retirement arrangements, endorsement management and the deployment of operational resources.
