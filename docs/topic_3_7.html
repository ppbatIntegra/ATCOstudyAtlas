ANSP perspective

Final Report · §5.2, By stakeholder perspective

The prioritisation set out in the previous section reflects the ANSP perspective, since the ANSP implements most of the measures and directly benefits from a reduced workforce gap. Closing the gap, however, also depends on actors beyond the ANSP.

This section therefore examines the full set of solutions from three complementary viewpoints, operational (ANSP), governance/policy, and technical, so each recommendation can be directed to the stakeholder able to act on it.

ANSP

In the previous sections, we have effectively filtered and prioritised the solutions from the ANSP perspective. The initial set of 78 solutions was reduced to 36 candidate recommendations through the application of the importance and feasibility criteria described previously. This analysis assesses these 36 candidate recommendations collectively, regardless of pillar, to identify the measures with the greatest overall potential to reduce future ATCO shortages from an ANSP perspective.

To do so, the candidate recommendations are first filtered based on the sensitivity analysis results. Priority is given to solutions affecting ATCO productivity and/or utilisation, as these variables were identified as the most influential controllable drivers of the ATCO gap. Among these, we prefer solutions that affect more than one model variable, as they can generate greater overall impact. This leaves four candidate recommendations. One of these additionally depends on enabling technologies and was therefore considered less feasible than the remaining measures.

Table 36: Global top three — ANSP (operational) perspective (Source: Consortium assessment)

IDName
TL-UT.BP.1Dedicated management of instructor roles and workloads
TL-UT.FP.2Adopt competency-based progression models
TL-L.BP.2Facilitate mobility through common operational language arrangements and digital licence management

The resulting top three recommendations all belong to the Training & Licensing pillar and correspond to the same measures previously identified as the highest-priority recommendations within that pillar. This outcome reinforces the conclusions of the preceding analysis: the greatest opportunity to improve future controller availability lies not in increasing recruitment alone, but in strengthening the efficiency, capacity and flexibility of the training and licensing pipeline.

The result is also fully consistent with the preliminary qualitative assessment and the root-cause analysis. From the ANSP perspective, Unit Training emerged as the most critical area, while training capacity, training throughput, and workforce flexibility were repeatedly identified as the principal constraints affecting future controller availability. The prioritisation process therefore confirms that measures targeting these issues offer the greatest potential to reduce the future ATCO gap and strengthen the long-term resilience of the European ATCO workforce system.

Governance perspective

Final Report · §5.2, Governance

The ANSP perspective presented in the previous section builds on the filtered set of 36 candidate recommendations. However, from a governance perspective, the objective is different. Rather than identifying the measures that ANSPs can implement directly, the purpose is to identify those solutions requiring support, coordination or action beyond the ANSP level. To do so, the filtering process is reset and the analysis returns to the full set of 78 solutions identified throughout the study.

The first filtering criterion consists of selecting all measures classified as Internal + External or Internal + External + Technology, resulting in a set of 29 solutions. The complete list is provided in Annex L.

It is important to note that these measures do not necessarily require the introduction of new rules / regulatory measures. In many cases, implementation depends on policy decisions, common procedures, guidance material, coordination mechanisms, or harmonised approaches developed by National Supervisory Authorities and Competent Authorities (NSAs/NCAs), EASA, the European Commission, or other European stakeholders. In some cases, successful implementation may also depend on cooperation between multiple ANSPs or cross-border coordination arrangements.

Further prioritisation can be done by retaining only solutions that address the most significant challenges identified throughout the study (i.e., those classified as High Importance). Applying this filter produces the following shortlist of measures where regulators should focus in order to reduce or remove the ATCO gap.

The resulting shortlist highlights a consistent theme. The most relevant measures requiring governance actions are concentrated around three areas: selection, training capacity and flexibility, and controller mobility. Several recommendations aim to improve the effectiveness of candidate assessment and harmonise selection approaches across Europe, while others seek to increase the training system's capacity and flexibility by expanding the pool of qualified and available instructors and introducing more competency-based training approaches. In parallel, many recommendations focus on reducing barriers to mobility through more uniform licensing practices, licence acceptance arrangements, and more flexible approaches to qualifications.

Taken together, these findings suggest that regulators and policy-makers' most valuable contribution is not necessarily creating new rules, but facilitating a more flexible, interoperable, and scalable ATCO workforce system. Supporting more harmonised implementation, enabling mobility, reducing unnecessary barriers, and fostering common approaches across ANSPs would address many of the challenges identified throughout the study and reinforce the effectiveness of the measures ANSPs implement directly.

Technical perspective

Final Report · §5.2, Technical

The governance/policy perspective presented in the previous section focuses on measures requiring policy, governance or institutional support. From a technical perspective, the objective is to identify solutions whose implementation depends on the development, deployment, or maturity of supporting technologies. To do so, the analysis returns to the full set of 78 solutions and selects all measures classified as Internal + Technology or Internal + External + Technology, resulting in a set of 13 solutions. The complete list is provided in Annex L.

These solutions do not necessarily require the development of entirely new technologies. In many cases, they rely on the deployment, integration, or wider adoption of existing technologies whose implementation remains limited, fragmented, or dependent on future development programmes. Consequently, this perspective is particularly relevant for technology providers, research programmes such as SESAR, and other actors involved in the evolution of the European ATM technological landscape.

Further prioritisation can be done by retaining only solutions that address the most significant challenges identified throughout the study (i.e., those classified as High Importance). Applying this filter produces the following shortlist of measures.

Table 38: Recommendations – Technical perspective (Source: Consortium assessment)

H-S.BP.4Incorporate behavioural and assessment-centre evaluation methods

O-O.FP.1Increase automation of workload-intensive operational tasks

The resulting shortlist shows that the most relevant technical interventions are concentrated around four complementary themes: improving candidate assessment, increasing workforce flexibility through more flexible approaches, enhancing operational productivity through automation, and enabling new operational concepts through virtualisation and remote service provision.

Taken together, these findings suggest that the technical community's greatest contribution lies in increasing the productivity, flexibility, and scalability of the ATM system. While technology alone cannot solve workforce shortages, solutions such as advanced selection tools, more flexible licensing approaches, automation of workload-intensive tasks, and virtual service provision can significantly increase the effectiveness of the existing workforce and reduce pressure on the training pipeline. As a result, technological development should be viewed as a key enabler of the long-term transformation of the European ATCO workforce system rather than as a standalone response to workforce shortages.
