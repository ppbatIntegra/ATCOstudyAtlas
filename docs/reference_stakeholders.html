Stakeholder management plan

Inception Report · Page 7

Changes to the rules governing the licensing and training of ATCOs affect more than 10,000 licensed controllers across Europe, not to mention the personnel involved in training and licensing activities at ANSPs, training organisations, national supervisory authorities, professional organisations, and European institutions.

Stakeholder mapping

Inception Report · Page 8

Stakeholder management begins by identifying all relevant groups and entities that could be affected by the changes. Once all stakeholders are identified and arranged in homogenous stakeholder groups, the resulting structure can be used to define the requir ed actions and specify various tools and channels to be used for engaging with each group.

Stakeholder perspectives

Final Report · §5.2, By stakeholder perspective

The prioritisation set out in the previous section reflects the ANSP perspective, since the ANSP implements most of the measures and directly benefits from a reduced workforce gap. Closing the gap, however, also depends on actors beyond the ANSP.

This section therefore examines the full set of solutions from three complementary viewpoints, operational (ANSP), governance/policy, and technical, so each recommendation can be directed to the stakeholder able to act on it.

It is important to note that these measures do not necessarily require the introduction of new rules / regulatory measures. In many cases, implementation depends on policy decisions, common procedures, guidance material, coordination mechanisms, or harmonised approaches developed by National Supervisory Authorities and Competent Authorities (NSAs/NCAs), EASA, the European Commission, or other European stakeholders. In some cases, successful implementation may also depend on cooperation between multiple ANSPs or cross-border coordination arrangements.

The resulting shortlist highlights a consistent theme. The most relevant measures requiring governance actions are concentrated around three areas: selection, training capacity and flexibility, and controller mobility. Several recommendations aim to improve the effectiveness of candidate assessment and harmonise selection approaches across Europe, while others seek to increase the training system's capacity and flexibility by expanding the pool of qualified and available instructors and introducing more competency-based training approaches. In parallel, many recommendations focus on reducing barriers to mobility through more uniform licensing practices, licence acceptance arrangements, and more flexible approaches to qualifications.

Taken together, these findings suggest that regulators and policy-makers' most valuable contribution is not necessarily creating new rules, but facilitating a more flexible, interoperable, and scalable ATCO workforce system. Supporting more harmonised implementation, enabling mobility, reducing unnecessary barriers, and fostering common approaches across ANSPs would address many of the challenges identified throughout the study and reinforce the effectiveness of the measures ANSPs implement directly.

The governance/policy perspective presented in the previous section focuses on measures requiring policy, governance or institutional support. From a technical perspective, the objective is to identify solutions whose implementation depends on the development, deployment, or maturity of supporting technologies. To do so, the analysis returns to the full set of 78 solutions and selects all measures classified as Internal + Technology or Internal + External + Technology, resulting in a set of 13 solutions. The complete list is provided in Annex L.

These solutions do not necessarily require the development of entirely new technologies. In many cases, they rely on the deployment, integration, or wider adoption of existing technologies whose implementation remains limited, fragmented, or dependent on future development programmes. Consequently, this perspective is particularly relevant for technology providers, research programmes such as SESAR, and other actors involved in the evolution of the European ATM technological landscape.
