About the Knowledge Atlas

A Knowledge Atlas organises a body of knowledge into connected topics. It lets readers choose a starting point, explore a question and follow links between the evidence, explanations, tools and recommendations that relate to it.

What purpose does it serve?

A substantial study can be relevant to many people for different reasons. A Knowledge Atlas helps each reader find the material that matters to their question while keeping its wider context within reach.

It makes relationships visible. A workforce challenge can be connected to the stage of the ATCO pipeline where it arises, the evidence used to understand it, the model variables it affects and the measures that could address it. Readers can explore those connections as their questions develop.

How does it differ from a linear report?

A report develops an argument in a deliberate sequence. Its chapters build from the objectives and methods through the evidence to the conclusions and recommendations. Reading that sequence gives a complete account of how the study reached its findings.

An Atlas offers several routes through the material. You can begin with an operational problem, a stage in the controller lifecycle, a proposed solution or the recommendations relevant to your role. Links lead to the explanations and evidence needed to understand that topic.

The study reports remain the reference for the complete analysis and its original context. The Atlas helps you navigate their material and return to the parts you need. Source annotations identify the report and the relevant chapter, annex or event slide.

Finding your way around this Atlas

The four Pathways provide guided routes through the ATCO pipeline, workforce shortages, challenges and solutions, and the future ATCO. Each route brings related topics into a reading order.

The thematic sections provide further ways into the same knowledge base.

  • The Missions section explains the study’s objectives and the questions it set out to answer.
  • The Our expeditions section explains the data analysis, consultation, modelling and other work behind the findings.
  • The Artefacts section introduces the pipeline, model, catalogue, proposed competence and licensing framework, and the Atlas itself.
  • The Discoveries and directions section presents findings and recommendations, including views for different audiences.
  • The Atlas Library section brings together information about the reports, analytical outputs, survey material, references and formal stakeholder events.

How to use it

  • Start with a question. Choose a Pathway that matches it, or use Discoveries and directions to begin with your role in the ATM community.
  • Read the page overview, then follow the connections that help answer your question. You can move between a specific issue and its place in the wider pipeline.
  • Explore the detail when you need it. Expand the sections in the historical analysis to follow the reasoning and graphs. Open an illustration to view it at full size.
  • Check the nature of the evidence. Historical analysis describes observed trends over a stated period. Model results explore future outcomes under assumptions. Proposed approaches such as competency-based licensing describe possible developments.
  • When considering measures, use the catalogue alongside its stakeholder perspectives and the local intervention portfolio discussion. Their relevance depends on the local situation and the challenges being addressed.
  • Use the source annotations and study source records to trace material back to its original context.
