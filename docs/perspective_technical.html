Recommendations

Final Report · §5.2, By stakeholder perspective: Technical

The governance/policy perspective presented in the previous section focuses on measures requiring policy, governance or institutional support. From a technical perspective, the objective is to identify solutions whose implementation depends on the development, deployment, or maturity of supporting technologies. To do so, the analysis returns to the full set of 78 solutions and selects all measures classified as Internal + Technology or Internal + External + Technology, resulting in a set of 13 solutions. The complete list is provided in Annex L.

These solutions do not necessarily require the development of entirely new technologies. In many cases, they rely on the deployment, integration, or wider adoption of existing technologies whose implementation remains limited, fragmented, or dependent on future development programmes. Consequently, this perspective is particularly relevant for technology providers, research programmes such as SESAR, and other actors involved in the evolution of the European ATM technological landscape.

Further prioritisation can be done by retaining only solutions that address the most significant challenges identified throughout the study (i.e., those classified as High Importance). Applying this filter produces the following shortlist of measures.

The resulting shortlist shows that the most relevant technical interventions are concentrated around four complementary themes: improving candidate assessment, increasing workforce flexibility through more flexible approaches, enhancing operational productivity through automation, and enabling new operational concepts through virtualisation and remote service provision.

Taken together, these findings suggest that the technical community's greatest contribution lies in increasing the productivity, flexibility, and scalability of the ATM system. While technology alone cannot solve workforce shortages, solutions such as advanced selection tools, more flexible licensing approaches, automation of workload-intensive tasks, and virtual service provision can significantly increase the effectiveness of the existing workforce and reduce pressure on the training pipeline. As a result, technological development should be viewed as a key enabler of the long-term transformation of the European ATCO workforce system rather than as a standalone response to workforce shortages.
