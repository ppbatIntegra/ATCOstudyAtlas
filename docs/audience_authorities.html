Licensing and endorsement

The logical model of the ATCO management cycle · Licensing (and endorsement)

However, while the licence itself is harmonised, endorsements and unit-specific validations remain largely local. States, through their Civil Aviation Administrations, retain responsibility for approving training organisations, defining local operational requirements, setting language proficiency conditions and overseeing unit endorsements. As a result, cross-recognition of operational privileges depends not only on ANSP practices, but also on how flexibly and consistently national authorities interpret and apply the regulatory framework. This makes endorsement and validation practices a key lever for enabling or constraining operational flexibility.

In this context, the role of States and Civil Aviation Administrations becomes particularly important. While European regulation provides the framework for harmonisation, progress toward more flexible and portable endorsement schemes largely depends on national authorities' willingness to adapt oversight practices, support cross-border cooperation, and accept new validation concepts that demonstrate safety. This requires a proactive, enabling regulatory approach, in which authorities actively drive change rather than merely approving established practices.

Language requirements and local operational rules will continue to form necessary boundary conditions, especially in complex or high-density environments. However, SESAR interoperability studies indicate that excessive localisation of endorsement requirements can significantly limit flexibility and increase training and validation overhead in cooperative or cross-border operations.

Taken together, these publications show that cross-recognition of ATCO licences already exists at the regulatory level, but that endorsement and validation practices remain the main limiting factor for operational flexibility. Enabling dynamic and cross-border airspace operations, therefore, depends not only on ANSP initiatives but also on States and Civil Aviation Administrations providing the regulatory flexibility and leadership needed to support new endorsement concepts. When national authorities actively facilitate alignment and mutual trust, endorsement schemes can evolve to support better adaptability, resilience and performance within the existing European licensing framework.

Governance perspective

Final Report · §5.2, Governance

It is important to note that these measures do not necessarily require the introduction of new rules / regulatory measures. In many cases, implementation depends on policy decisions, common procedures, guidance material, coordination mechanisms, or harmonised approaches developed by National Supervisory Authorities and Competent Authorities (NSAs/NCAs), EASA, the European Commission, or other European stakeholders. In some cases, successful implementation may also depend on cooperation between multiple ANSPs or cross-border coordination arrangements.

The resulting shortlist highlights a consistent theme. The most relevant measures requiring governance actions are concentrated around three areas: selection, training capacity and flexibility, and controller mobility. Several recommendations aim to improve the effectiveness of candidate assessment and harmonise selection approaches across Europe, while others seek to increase the training system's capacity and flexibility by expanding the pool of qualified and available instructors and introducing more competency-based training approaches. In parallel, many recommendations focus on reducing barriers to mobility through more uniform licensing practices, licence acceptance arrangements, and more flexible approaches to qualifications.

Taken together, these findings suggest that regulators and policy-makers' most valuable contribution is not necessarily creating new rules, but facilitating a more flexible, interoperable, and scalable ATCO workforce system. Supporting more harmonised implementation, enabling mobility, reducing unnecessary barriers, and fostering common approaches across ANSPs would address many of the challenges identified throughout the study and reinforce the effectiveness of the measures ANSPs implement directly.

Approval and oversight

Final Report · Chapter 7, Conceptual and licensing benefits

On the one hand, the CBL concept requires specifying competencies in all three domains and defining the appropriate performance standards and observable behaviours. However, competent authorities and NSAs can set the standards and observable behaviours for the specific ATS units, and update these as necessary in line with the evolution of the technical and operational environment. This also applies at the competency level: even if the list of competencies needs revision or updating, the overall structure and licensing logic remain the same, creating much-needed continuity and stability in the system.

Finally, shifting oversight and approval toward competence requirements and their assessment, rather than training courses and materials, may also reduce administrative burden.
