Assessing challenges

Final Report · §4.2 and §4.3, Assessment of key areas

The root-cause analysis focuses on the areas of the ATCO management cycle most likely to influence future controller availability. Whilst all key areas contribute to the future balance between ATCO demand and supply, their relative importance differs considerably. To identify the areas requiring the most attention, we assess evidence from interviews and surveys using two complementary indicators: Incidence and Severity. The criteria used for this assessment are summarised in the following table.

Building on the prioritisation established in the previous section, this section examines each key area in detail to identify the specific challenges affecting ATCO availability and the measures capable of addressing them. The assessment follows a common structure across all pillars and key areas: the challenges reported by stakeholders through interviews and surveys are presented first, followed by the potential solutions. Each challenge and solution has a unique code, described in Annex H, which provides the corresponding detailed tables, so findings remain comparable across the lifecycle and can be linked to the model's quantitative framework.

Solutions are drawn from the consultation process, the literature review and the wider evidence collected throughout the study, and are grouped into two categories:

  • Best Practices (BP), corresponding to measures that are already being implemented by one or more ANSPs and for which practical evidence of application exists; and
  • Future Practices (FP), corresponding to measures, concepts or proposals that have been identified as potentially beneficial but for which no current implementation has been identified within the consultation sample.

Sensitivity analysis

Final Report · §4.4, Sensitivity analysis

In addition to understanding the root causes of workforce shortages, it is necessary to assess which interventions have the greatest potential to reduce the future ATCO gap. Since each challenge and solution identified throughout the study ultimately affects one or more variables of the supply-demand model, understanding the relative importance of these variables provides an objective basis for prioritising solutions and recommendations.

The purpose of this section is therefore to determine which model variables have the greatest influence on the future ATCO gap and, consequently, which areas offer the greatest potential for intervention.

The variables included in the model affect the ATCO gap in different ways. Understanding these differences is important for interpreting the sensitivity analysis, as not all variables have the same type or timing of effect. Three main mechanisms can be distinguished.

  • Multiplicative variables (demand side): ATCO demand is calculated by dividing the number of flight hours controlled by productivity, maximum staff hours and utilisation, as explained in Annex F. These three variables therefore directly influence the number of controllers required. For example, higher productivity means that each controller can handle more traffic, reducing the number of controllers needed. The same applies to maximum staff hours and utilisation. The effect is not fully symmetrical, meaning that a decrease in one of these variables increases the workforce requirement by more than an equivalent increase reduces it.
  • Flow variables (supply side): Intake, training success rate and attrition affect the number of controllers entering and leaving the operational workforce each year. Their impact therefore builds up over time. An increase in intake or training success rate results in more controllers eventually reaching operations, while higher attrition reduces the workforce. However, these changes do not have an immediate effect, as new recruits need to complete the training process before becoming ATCOs.
  • Training pipeline variables: Training capacity and training duration determine how effectively new recruits can progress through the training pipeline. Training capacity limits the number of trainees that can be processed, meaning that increasing intake or training success rate will have a limited effect if sufficient training capacity is not available. Training duration also determines how quickly recruits can reach operations and, in the model, affects the number of trainees that can be processed through a given number of training places. Reducing training duration can therefore improve the speed of the pipeline, although it does not by itself increase the number of controllers if other constraints remain unchanged.

Prioritising solutions

Final Report · Chapter 5, Summary of Solutions and Recommendations

Once the full set of potential measures was identified and defined, the 78 candidate solutions were prioritised against two criteria to isolate those best able to reduce the future ATCO gap: the importance of the challenges addressed and the feasibility of implementation. The detailed prioritisation criteria are presented in Annex I.

Applying these criteria reduced the initial set of 78 solutions to a shortlist of 36 candidate recommendations. The full assessment is attached in Annex J. These constitute the starting point for the recommendations presented in this section per pillar and are subsequently assessed from different stakeholder perspectives.

Per pillars

To facilitate assessment, we first organised the 36 candidate recommendations according to the four pillars of the ATCO pipeline used throughout the study: Transversal, Hiring, Training & Licensing, and Operational. The complete list of candidate recommendations, together with the challenges addressed, implementation horizon, degree of control and affected model variables, is provided in Annex K.

The following subsections describe the rationale for prioritising solutions within each pillar and present the recommendations ultimately retained. This approach ensures consistency with the analytical framework applied throughout the study whilst allowing the most relevant measures to be identified within each area of the ATCO management cycle.
