Participation and shared learning

Final Report · Executive Summary · Recommendations
  • ANSPs should also engage in regular, structured discussions around the topics addressed in the study to share experience, knowledge, and practices, and to ensure that the community can learn from individual efforts and ideas.
  • NSAs, ANSPs, PSOs and other state-level stakeholders involved in the planning of ANSP staffing and ATCO training and licensing should engage in regular, structured discussions around the topics addressed in the study and define ways of addressing the local ATCO gap as early as practically possible, and to ensure that all stakeholders share an understanding of the urgency of the issue, the underlying locally specific root causes, and the possible solutions.

Training and assessment

Final Report · §7.6 · Conceptual and framework benefits

Defining requirements across all three competence domains can help trace the capabilities needed for an ATS unit back to the aptitudes, knowledge, skills and attitudes developed through selection and training. The six-layer competence model explains these relationships.

The three domains provide a shared structure for defining training requirements, objectives and phases. This is particularly relevant where training is partly outsourced or liberalised, and when defining transitional, pre-OJT and OJT content.

Finally, we also consider the notion that the concept is a natural extension of the CBTA methodology, offering a conceptual benefit by making socialising the concept easier and implementation smoother (if and when that happens).

Beyond direct application to ATCO training and licensing, the CBL concept could also be rolled out for instructors, assessors, and potentially other roles. On the one hand, instructors require a specific set of pedagogical competencies, and assessors also require specific competencies to perform their tasks. These could serve as the basis for instructor and assessor endorsements. On the other hand, if we consider how training may be transformed by the three-domain approach, separate instructor roles may be defined for the specific domains (at least to a certain extent). Depending on whether and how this becomes possible, different requirements for the specific instructor roles may help alleviate the shortage of operational instructors, which ANSPs reported as a major constraint during stakeholder discussions.

Contributing to the future framework

Final Report · §7.7 · Next steps

The CBL concept offers a potential route to future-proofing training and licensing. Further work is needed to define its detailed procedures and requirements.

We understand that the proposed CBL concept could feed into EASA’s projected rulemaking task RMT.0738, and could be progressed through that process. However, we also recognise the need to raise awareness about the concept and start educating the wider community and socialise the idea, so that experts involved in ATCO training and licensing have ample time to consider it and provide their thoughts and contributions to the regulatory work. We also understand that even before the rulemaking task may start, DG MOVE will have to specify in more detail the objectives and expected outcomes of the process.
