Main conclusions

Final Report · Chapter 6 · Conclusions

The analysis conducted throughout this study confirms that ATCO shortages remain one of the most significant challenges facing the European ATM network. Although the severity of the problem varies between ANSPs and operational environments, the evidence collected demonstrates that workforce shortages are neither temporary nor attributable to a single cause. Instead, they are a structural challenge resulting from the interaction of multiple factors across the entire ATCO management cycle.

The quantitative assessment shows that the imbalance between workforce demand and workforce availability already existed before the COVID-19 pandemic, was temporarily masked by the collapse in traffic demand, and re-emerged rapidly during the subsequent recovery period. The analysis further indicates that this imbalance is likely to persist throughout the coming decade. Even under favourable assumptions regarding recruitment and productivity improvements, workforce pressures remain visible across much of the projection horizon. Although highly optimistic scenarios indicate the gap may eventually close by 2036, this outcome still implies about ten years of sustained workforce constraints, with the period around 2027-2028 appearing particularly critical.

A key finding of the study is that ATCO shortages cannot be explained solely through the current licensing framework. The evidence collected through interviews, surveys and modelling demonstrates that workforce availability is shaped by decisions and constraints occurring throughout the entire ATCO management cycle. Workforce planning, recruitment, selection, training, licensing, operational deployment, workforce mobility, and workforce utilisation all shape the future balance between ATCO demand and supply. Consequently, no single intervention can resolve the challenge in isolation.

Within this broader lifecycle, Unit Training emerged as the most critical area of concern. It was the only key area classified as high in both incidence and severity, reflecting both the frequency with which it was raised by stakeholders and the extent to which it affects ANSPs across the network. Instructor availability, On-The-Job Training capacity and training throughput were consistently identified as the principal bottlenecks limiting the production of new operational ATCOs. More generally, the findings suggest that the key challenge is not attracting candidates into the profession, but rather converting those candidates into operationally qualified ATCOs.

The study also highlights the importance of long-term workforce planning. Given the length of the training process, decisions made today often shape operational capacity years into the future. As a result, workforce shortages frequently reflect historical planning decisions rather than current recruitment activity. This reinforces the need for workforce planning approaches that can anticipate future operational requirements and align recruitment and training capacity accordingly.

The quantitative analysis shows that future workforce requirements depend not only on traffic growth, but also on productivity achieved in the operational environment. Whilst traffic demand remains an important driver, the model shows that productivity assumptions significantly influence the future evolution of workforce demand. Under the scenarios assessed, the most realistic trajectory lies between a situation in which productivity remains broadly unchanged and one in which historical productivity improvements continue over time. The analysis therefore suggests that improvements in operational efficiency will be as important as workforce growth in addressing future shortages.

The sensitivity analysis confirms that not all variables influence the future ATCO gap to the same extent. Among the controllable model variables, ATCO productivity, utilisation and maximum staff hours have the greatest impact on the future workforce balance. On the supply side, training success rates and recruitment intake represent the most influential variables. Training capacity and training duration play a different but equally important role, acting as enabling conditions that determine how improvements in recruitment and training performance can ultimately translate into additional operational ATCOs.

The analysis of mobility and licensing also highlights a key distinction between regulatory flexibility and practical workforce mobility. Whilst stakeholders generally support greater mobility across the European ATM network, the evidence suggests the main barriers are not the licence itself, but the operational realities of local procedures, unit-specific training, airspace characteristics, and language requirements. Improving workforce mobility therefore requires a broader perspective than licensing harmonisation alone.

Taken together, the findings suggest that maintaining current trends will not be sufficient to restore long-term equilibrium between ATCO demand and supply. Active intervention is required, particularly in those parts of the lifecycle that most directly influence the production and deployment of operational ATCOs. The prioritisation exercise confirms this conclusion: the highest-ranked recommendations are concentrated within the Training & Licensing pillar and aim to increase training effectiveness, improve workforce flexibility, and strengthen the transition from trainee to ATCO.

In particular, three measures consistently emerged as the most promising interventions:

  • Establish dedicated management of instructor roles and workloads ;
  • Adopt competency-based progression models; and
  • Facilitate mobility through common operational language arrangements and digital licence management.

These recommendations reflect a broader conclusion of the study: future ATCO shortages will not be resolved through recruitment measures alone. The greatest opportunity lies in strengthening the efficiency of the training process, increasing workforce flexibility and improving the operational utilisation of qualified ATCOs.

Finally, the study highlights that addressing the workforce challenge requires coordinated action across the ATM ecosystem. ANSPs remain the primary actors responsible for implementing many of the identified measures, but regulators and technology providers also play a critical role in enabling improvements related to licensing, mobility, automation and future operational concepts. Long-term workforce sustainability will therefore depend on a combination of organisational, regulatory and technological initiatives working towards a common objective: ensuring that the European ATM network can continue to provide safe, efficient and resilient air traffic services in the face of future demand.

Recommendations to the community

Final Report · Executive Summary · Recommendations

Based on the analyses, findings, and conclusions of the study, we invite the various stakeholders of the European ATM community to consider the following recommendations.

  • ANSPs should explore the root-cause analysis and the solution catalogue to identify practices applicable to them, identify the most effective local measures for closing the ATCO gap, and implement them as soon as practically possible.
  • ANSPs should also engage in regular, structured discussions around the topics addressed in the study to share experience, knowledge, and practices, and to ensure that the community can learn from individual efforts and ideas.
  • NSAs, ANSPs, PSOs and other state-level stakeholders involved in the planning of ANSP staffing and ATCO training and licensing should engage in regular, structured discussions around the topics addressed in the study and define ways of addressing the local ATCO gap as early as practically possible, and to ensure that all stakeholders share an understanding of the urgency of the issue, the underlying locally specific root causes, and the possible solutions.
  • The European Commission should support regular, structured discussions within the ATM community and among the state-level stakeholders involved in staffing, training and licensing. This could involve providing suitable platforms or encouraging better use of existing ones.
  • The European Commission, together with EASA, and the Network Manager, and operational stakeholders, is recommended to define ways for monitoring the actions taken and solutions implemented by ANSPs and other relevant stakeholders in addressing ATCO shortages, and to follow the evolution of the ATCO gap regularly.
  • The European Commission is recommended to consider competency-based licensing as the potential future architecture for ATCO licensing. EASA is recommended to consider competency-based licensing in the context of its RMT.0738.
  • EASA is recommended to consider findings from the study related to the ATCO training licensing framework in the context of its RMT.0668-5.
  • The European Commission, EASA, Member States, and NCAs/NSAs are invited to explore, especially the governance view of, the solution catalogue and identify items where they could support addressing the issues with governance, policy, and regulatory actions.
  • The European Commission, the SESAR 3 Joint Undertaking, and the SESAR Deployment Manager are invited to explore the availability of technical developments enabling the solutions indicated in this report. To this end, the institutions involved should define the development, industrialisation and deployment paths leading to further efficiencies and availability of more powerful tools for ATCOs.
  • We recommend that the European Commission consider the study's findings and examine how the performance and charging scheme of the SES may support Member States and ANSPs in addressing existing issues, and how the findings may be taken forward in the context of RP5 preparations.
