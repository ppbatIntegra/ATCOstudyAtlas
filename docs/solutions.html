From root causes to solutions

Final Report · Executive Summary · From root causes to solutions

The analysis demonstrates that workforce challenges exist across all stages of the ATCO management cycle, but are particularly concentrated within Unit Training, which emerged as the area of greatest concern among participating ANSPs. Constraints related to training capacity, training duration and training success rates were consistently identified as the main bottlenecks limiting the ability of organisations to convert recruitment efforts into operationally qualified controllers. More broadly, the findings confirm that workforce shortages result from multiple interconnected challenges across the ATCO management cycle and therefore require coordinated interventions spanning recruitment, training, licensing and operational deployment.

To identify the most effective responses, the study assessed both existing practices implemented by European ANSPs and future opportunities identified through literature review and stakeholder consultation. In total, 78 solutions were mapped against the workforce challenges they address and the variables influencing the ATCO gap. These solutions were subsequently prioritised according to their importance and implementation feasibility, before being refined through the sensitivity analysis of the supply-demand model. This process reduced the initial longlist to 36 candidate recommendations, representing the measures with the strongest combination of potential impact and practical deliverability.

The prioritisation exercise provides an important insight into the nature of the workforce challenge. Whilst increasing recruitment intake is often perceived as the primary response to ATCO shortages, both stakeholder consultation and modelling results indicate that the principal constraint lies elsewhere. ANSPs consistently highlighted limitations in the training process rather than difficulties in attracting candidates, and the model confirms that improvements in recruitment alone have a comparatively limited impact unless they are accompanied by higher training success rates and a greater capacity to progress trainees towards operational qualification.

As a result, the Training & Licensing pillar emerged as the highest priority area for intervention. Notably, the three highest-ranked recommendations from the ANSP perspective are all associated with this pillar, with two focused on Unit Training, previously identified as the most critical area of the lifecycle, and one on Licensing:

  • Establish dedicated management of instructor roles and workloads;
  • Adopt competency-based progression models; and
  • Facilitate mobility through common operational language arrangements and digital licence management.

Together, these measures aim to increase training effectiveness, improve workforce flexibility and accelerate the transition from trainee to ATCO.

The sensitivity analysis further demonstrates that the variables with the greatest influence on the projected ATCO gap are predominantly operational in nature. Among the controllable factors, ATCO productivity, utilisation and maximum staff hours emerge as the most influential levers, followed by training success rates and recruitment intake. Training capacity and training duration also play a critical enabling role, determining the extent to which improvements in recruitment and training performance can ultimately be translated into additional ATCOs.

The analysis also highlights the scale of the challenge. Under the baseline scenario, eliminating the projected ATCO gap by 2036 would require substantial improvements in the most influential variables, in many cases exceeding 10%. This demonstrates that no single measure is likely to be sufficient and reinforces the need for a coordinated portfolio of interventions addressing multiple stages of the workforce lifecycle.

While ANSPs remain the primary actors responsible for implementing many of the identified measures and must seize every opportunity to address the ATCO shortage, the study also highlights the important role of regulators and technology providers. Several high-impact solutions, particularly those related to licensing, mobility, automation, and advanced operational concepts, require governance and policy support and technology deployment to deliver their full benefits. Reducing future workforce shortages will therefore depend on coordinated action across operational, regulatory and technical stakeholders.

Overall, the study concludes that future ATCO shortages can be mitigated, but not through recruitment measures alone. The greatest opportunity lies in strengthening the efficiency and effectiveness of the training process, increasing workforce flexibility and implementing operational practices and technologies that enhance the productivity and utilisation of qualified controllers.
